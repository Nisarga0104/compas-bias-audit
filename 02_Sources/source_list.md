# Sources — COMPAS Bias Audit

1. **Angwin, J., Larson, J., Mattu, S., & Kirchner, L. (2016). "Machine Bias." ProPublica, May 23, 2016.**
   https://www.propublica.org/article/machine-bias-risk-assessments-in-criminal-sentencing
   Use: Primary source — the original investigation, dataset, and headline finding.

2. **Larson, J. et al. "How We Analyzed the COMPAS Recidivism Algorithm." ProPublica, 2016.**
   (ProPublica's own methodology page — same domain as source 1.)
   Use: Primary source for the exact dataset size, filtering criteria, and statistical method
   ProPublica used — cite this rather than secondary summaries for any specific number.

3. **Dieterich, W., Mendoza, C., & Brennan, T. (2016). "COMPAS Risk Scales: Demonstrating
   Accuracy Equity and Predictive Parity." Northpointe Inc. Research Department, July 8, 2016.**
   Use: Primary source — the vendor's own rebuttal and its accuracy-equity / predictive-parity
   argument.

4. **Kleinberg, J., Mullainathan, S., & Raghavan, M. (2016). "Inherent Trade-Offs in the Fair
   Determination of Risk Scores." arXiv:1609.05807.**
   https://arxiv.org/abs/1609.05807
   Use: Primary academic source for the fairness-impossibility theorem — cite directly when
   explaining *why* ProPublica and Northpointe could both be right on their own terms.

5. **Chouldechova, A. (2017). "Fair Prediction with Disparate Impact: A Study of Bias in
   Recidivism Prediction Instruments." Big Data, 5(2).**
   Use: Secondary academic confirmation of the same impossibility result, framed specifically
   around the COMPAS case — useful for a more accessible explanation than Kleinberg et al.

6. **Flores, A. W., Lowenkamp, C. T., & Bechtel, K. (2016). "False Positives, False Negatives,
   and False Analyses: A Rejoinder to 'Machine Bias.'" Federal Probation, 80(2).**
   Use: A second, independent rejoinder to ProPublica — useful for showing the debate wasn't
   just "vendor vs. journalist" but had independent academic voices on both sides.

## Note on what's still unverified
The "~137 input variables" figure widely repeated in secondary sources (blogs, law reviews)
could not be confirmed against a Northpointe/Equivant primary document in this pass — their
full scoring methodology is proprietary. If this number stays in the final draft, attribute it
as "commonly reported" rather than as independently confirmed.
