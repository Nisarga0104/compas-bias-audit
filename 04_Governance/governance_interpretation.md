# Governance Interpretation

## Finding 1 — Fairness is metric-dependent

The case demonstrates that "fairness" is not one universal statistic. Different metrics formalize different ethical and operational priorities.

## Finding 2 — Calibration alone is not a complete governance standard

A system can be calibrated while different groups experience materially different error rates. In a high-stakes context, governance should therefore consider both statistical properties and the consequences experienced by affected people.

## Finding 3 — Base rates require contextual investigation

Observed base-rate differences are relevant to interpreting fairness metrics. They should not automatically be treated as proof of either model bias or model fairness. An organization should investigate data-generating processes and possible upstream sources of disparity.

## Finding 4 — Governance must precede deployment

Fairness criteria should be selected and documented before deployment, not chosen after seeing which metric produces the most favorable result.

## Transferable governance principle

For a new high-stakes AI system, define the relevant harms first, map those harms to appropriate fairness metrics, document the trade-offs, obtain accountable approval, and monitor subgroup outcomes over time.
